I'm an 85-year-old clinical psychologist. My beautiful wife, Jolie, just underwent a second lumpectomy for lobular breast cancer. We went to Stanford Medical School and had a lumpectomy with Dr. Frederick Derbus. He did a fantastic job. What our job was during and after and before the procedure was to keep our spirits as high as possible, to maintain a fantastically positive attitude, a can-do attitude, an attitude that we know we're gonna be successful, and an attitude that says we're gonna live every moment to the fullest regardless of what comes down the pike with this cancer, and that's what we did. In fact, after I picked her up at the medical hospital at Stanford, we went to the Rodin Museum at Stanford University, and we had a wonderful museum walk, and after that, we went to a great Greek restaurant named Taverna. No matter what you're dealing with, including cancer, you can maintain a positive attitude because after all, what's the benefit of having a lousy attitude if you're having trouble physically?
