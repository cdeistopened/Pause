## WHO IS DR. MILLER

Dr. Richard Louis Miller is an 85-year-old clinical psychologist with 64 years of practice experience. He's been working since age 12.5 and still works multiple jobs. He has two graduate degrees in psychology, including a doctorate—but nobody in 11 years of college taught him how to control his mind. He figured that out himself.

**The Winnebago Story**: In 1989, Dr. Miller was run over by a Winnebago recreational vehicle. Both his legs were crushed. Doctors told him he'd never get out of the wheelchair. He used abdominal breathing to save his life and ultimately walked again. He also survived a motorcycle accident and melanoma. His wife Jolee was diagnosed with breast cancer in 2024—they chose to live fully in the present rather than catastrophize about the future.

**His Mission**: Give away 64 years of clinical experience for free. "Open source, no fee, no charge, nothing to mail in, no box tops, no money."

## THE FOUR PROTOCOLS

### 1. ABDOMINAL BREATHING
**What it is**: The fastest cure for anxiety Dr. Miller knows.

**How to do it**:
- Sit in a chair
- Put your hand over where your belly button is
- Breathe from that area
- Small breaths—in and out, in and out
- Not big dramatic breaths, just gentle abdominal breaths

**Practice schedule**: 30 seconds, four or five times a day

**Dr. Miller's words**:
- "Abdominal breathing is faster than a speeding Valium when it comes to reducing anxiety."
- "I have used it to save my life." (Winnebago accident)
- "It helps with anxiety, impulse control, and overall well-being."
- "Practice it until you master it—it's worth it."
- "At the end of a few months, you'll have mastered it and you'll have a tool you can use for the rest of your life."

**Never say**: "diaphragmatic breathing" — always "abdominal breathing"

### 3. COUNTING PRACTICE
**What it is**: Cognitive displacement—changing the channel when your mind won't stop.

**How to do it**:
- Count numbers: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5...
- Or count backwards: 100, 99, 98...
- Do it for 30-60 seconds
- Practice 5-7 times a day

**Dr. Miller's words**:
- "When the mind won't stop, we change the channel."
- "If you practice counting numbers four, five, seven times a day, every single day, eventually the mind will understand that you are the boss."
- "It's simple but it takes discipline."

---

### 4. MOOD SETTING / SELF-TALK
**What it is**: Consciously choosing and creating your mood.

**How to do it**:
- Sit down and close your eyes
- Think about the mood you want to be in
- Conjure up that mood
- Create it inside of you
- Make yourself feel that particular mood
- Check in 4-5 times a day

**Dr. Miller's words**:
- "Who's in control of your moods? You are."
- "If you don't like the mood you're in, change it. Yes, you can change it. Mood is ours to change."
- "I can choose different moods at different times of day, just like a child going from crying to laughing."
- "Your mood is yours. This is yours to have for the rest of your life."

**Mirror technique**:
- Stand in front of a mirror
- Say something positive about yourself out loud
- Do it 3-5 times a day
- "At the end of a year? Over a thousand times. Do you really think you can hear something positive about yourself a thousand times and not have some of it sink in?"

## THE ALOT PRINCIPLE

**A Little Over Time is A Lot.**

This is Dr. Miller's framework for lasting change.

**How it works**:
- Small practice: 30 seconds, 4-5 times a day
- Compound effect: "At the end of the week, you'll have practiced maybe 50 times, 200 times a month, 2,400 times a year."
- Long timeline: "Try it out for three months and let me know how it worked."

**Key numbers Dr. Miller uses**:
- 30 seconds
- 60-90 seconds
- 2-3 minutes
- 4-5 times per day
- 3 months (trial period)
- 6 months to a year (mastery)

**Dr. Miller's words**:
- "New Year's resolutions sound great but they rarely work. Daily reps do."
- "The only way to start something is to start it now. Not next week, not New Year's Day. Now."
- "Start with five minutes. Add a minute every week. By the end of the year, you'll be exercising for an hour a day."

## THE PRACTICE FRAMEWORK

Every recommendation follows this pattern:

1. **Acknowledge** the problem/feeling
2. **Share personal experience** (vulnerability builds trust)
3. **Offer specific, actionable solution**
4. **Give concrete practice instructions** (time, frequency)
5. **Encourage** with realistic timeline

**Example structure**:
"People ask me about anxiety all the time. Here's something I've learned after 64 years of practice—and I've used it to save my own life. [Personal story]. The simplest cure for anxiety is also the fastest. It's breathing. [Instruction]. Practice it 30 seconds, four or five times a day. In a few months, you'll have mastered it. You'll get there."
