- [**Chapter 4: Learning Diaphragmatic Breathing (36:30 – 47:30)**](https://www.notion.so/Chapter-4-Learning-Diaphragmatic-Breathing-36-30-47-30-19fbe9d00a4280bd9d71e8519e065ccf?pvs=21)
    
    Now, let’s dive into the heart of today’s lesson: diaphragmatic breathing, also known as abdominal breathing. This is the single most important tool in my arsenal for managing stress and anxiety—because when you control your breathing, you essentially control your state of mind.
    
    Here’s how it works:
    
    I begin by explaining that while our bodies naturally engage in involuntary breathing, that kind of breathing isn’t always enough when we’re under stress. Instead, we need to tap into voluntary breathing to keep our system stable, ensuring our body gets a steady flow of oxygen—the fuel that powers everything we do.
    
    To practice, start by sitting comfortably with both feet planted firmly on the floor. Place one hand lightly on your belly button and the other on your chest. When you take a slow, deep breath in, your focus should be on expanding your abdomen—watch as the hand on your belly rises gently. Your chest should remain as still as possible, with minimal movement. This is the key: you’re engaging your diaphragm, not just taking shallow, chest-based breaths.
    
    If you’re in a safe environment, close your eyes. Doing so helps you turn off external distractions and focus on the sensations inside your body—almost like watching an “inner TV” that shows you exactly what’s happening with your breath.
    
    It’s important not to overdo it. You don’t need to take giant, exaggerated breaths. In fact, taking too much air too quickly can lead to hyperventilation and even dizziness. Instead, aim for controlled breaths lasting about 30 to 90 seconds. I recommend practicing this drill multiple times a day—starting with at least five sessions daily, and then gradually increasing to ten if it feels right. With consistent practice, this method becomes almost automatic, even in high-stress situations.
    
    I also demonstrated a useful technique to “find” your diaphragm. By gently pushing out and then pulling in your abdominal muscles, you can feel exactly where you’re breathing from. This awareness helps reinforce the proper technique every time you practice.
    
    In essence, diaphragmatic breathing is more than just a relaxation exercise—it’s a foundational tool that equips you to maintain calm and control in the midst of chaos. Whether you’re facing a stressful meeting or an unexpected moment of panic, this technique will help you keep your system stable and your mind clear.
    
    Now, let’s practice together. Follow these steps, feel the gentle expansion of your abdomen, and let your diaphragm do its work. Ready? Let’s begin.
