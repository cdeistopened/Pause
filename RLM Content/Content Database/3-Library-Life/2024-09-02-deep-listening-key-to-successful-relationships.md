## Caption

Want to strengthen your relationship? Start with this one skill: deep listening.
After six decades of counseling couples, I've learned that deep listening is the most important skill in any relationship. It's more than just hearing—it's truly understanding and empathizing. When you listen without interrupting, without rushing to speak, and try to see from the other’s perspective, you build a stronger connection. Remember, a real partnership is about two people listening to each other.

## Transcript

After counseling couples for six decades, I found that there's one skill that's more important than any other. It's deep listening. There is a difference between just hearing and truly listening. Listening is when we sit back, we take in what the other person is saying, we try to put ourselves in their shoes. What are they thinking? What are they feeling when they're saying what they're saying? That is called empathy. Try to look at it from the other person's point of view. When you find yourself interrupting, that means you're not listening. When you sit back quietly, without interrupting, focusing on the other person, trying to understand what they're saying, summarizing back for them at different points what you think they said, checking for accuracy, that's how you deeply listen. And what's so important is that when our partners sense that we're really listening, they feel more heard. They feel more connected. Then you feel like you have a real partnership, not two people talking at each other. It's two people listening to one another.
